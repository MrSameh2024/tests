Part 1 · Introduction & Interview

Part 1 — Familiar Topics

Suggested duration: about 4–5 minutes. Answer naturally in 2–4 sentences where appropriate.

1. What do you do — do you work or are you a student?
2. What do you enjoy most about your work or studies?
3. How important is English in your daily life?
4. What kind of place do you prefer for studying or working?
5. Do you usually plan your day in advance?
6. What changes would make your local area better for young people?

Part 2 · Long Turn

Part 2 — Cue Card

You have 1 minute to prepare and should speak for 1–2 minutes.

Describe a skill you learned that has been useful in your life.

You should say:

  • what the skill is
  • when and how you learned it
  • how you use it now
  • and explain why this skill has been useful to you

Part 3 · Discussion

Part 3 — Abstract Discussion

Suggested duration: about 4–5 minutes. Develop ideas, compare viewpoints and give reasons/examples.

1. Why do some people learn new skills more successfully than others?
2. Should schools spend more time teaching practical life skills?
3. How has technology changed the way adults learn new skills?
4. Do employers value qualifications more than practical ability?
5. What kinds of skills may become more important in the future?

Diagnostic Self-Review

Listen back to your recordings before completing this review. This is a structured self-diagnostic, not an official IELTS band score.

Fluency & Coherence

Can you speak at length with logical development, limited hesitation and clear linking?

Lexical Resource

Can you use a sufficient range of vocabulary and paraphrase when needed?

Grammar Range & Accuracy

Can you use a mix of simple and complex structures with reasonable control?

Pronunciation

Is your speech generally clear, with understandable stress, rhythm and sounds?
